\section*{\Huge \textbf{Abstract}}

\vspace{0.5cm}

In this thesis, we study the problem of how to effectively share the buffer pool memory in a 
multi-tenant caching problem, proposing a dynamic buffer allocation approach that aims to 
minimize the fault rate of the cache while ensuring fairness among tenants and avoiding 
waste of resources, assuming that we can have reasonable seasonality recommendations for 
the cache size of each tenant.

We propose a general caching algorithm that can be used with different tenant selection 
policies and cache eviction policies. Additionally, we propose a tenant selection policy 
based on the cache fault rate and compare it to a baseline naive tenant selection policy 
based on the cache size recommendations.

We adapt several eviction policies to the multi-tenant scenario, analyzing and comparing 
their performance when combined with our tenant selection policy. The eviction policies 
considered are LRU, LFU, LRU-2, 2Q, MQ, LIRS, and LRFU, with LRU-2, LIRS, and MQ 
demonstrating the best results.

We evaluate our solutions on two real-world datasets, Brightkite and CitiBike, demonstrating 
the robustness and versatility of our approach. We aim to generalize the results by using 
the same hyperparameters across different datasets and performing minimal hyperparameter 
tuning.
